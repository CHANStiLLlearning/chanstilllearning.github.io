Smart Rules for Financing Your Next Vehicle

Automobile purchases represent one of the fastest depreciating assets the average consumer purchases. A brand-new car loses roughly 20% of its market value in year one and approximately 15% each subsequent year.

The 20/4/10 Rule of Thumb

Financial planners frequently recommend the 20/4/10 framework to protect yourself from becoming car-poor:

  • 20% Down Payment: Protects you from negative equity ("underwater" on your loan) as soon as you drive off the lot.
  • 4-Year Maximum Loan Term: Never finance a depreciating car for 72 or 84 months. If you cannot afford the 48-month payment, the car is too expensive for your current budget.
  • 10% Total Transportation Cap: Your loan payment, insurance premium, and fuel should consume less than 10% of your gross monthly income.

Dealer Financing vs. Credit Union Pre-Approval

Before stepping onto a dealership lot, always seek pre-approval from a local credit union or online bank. Dealers often mark up financing interest rates by 1.5% to 2.5% as pure profit. Walking in with a pre-approved interest rate forces the dealership to match or beat your outside offer.
