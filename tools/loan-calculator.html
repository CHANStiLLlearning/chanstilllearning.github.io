Understanding How Amortization Works

When you take out a fixed-rate installment loan (such as a mortgage or auto loan), your monthly payment remains the same, but the distribution between principal and interest changes dramatically over time.

Front-Loaded Interest Explained

During the early years of your loan, the remaining principal balance is at its highest. As a result, the majority of every monthly payment goes directly toward paying interest to the lender. As the principal drops, progressively more of your payment attacks the principal balance.

How to Save Thousands in Loan Interest

  • Make Bi-Weekly Payments: Making half-payments every two weeks results in 26 half-payments per year (equivalent to 13 full payments instead of 12), cutting years off a 30-year mortgage.
  • Round Up Your Monthly Payment: Adding just $100 or $200 extra directly earmarked for "Principal Only" reduces lifetime interest substantially.
  • Refinance When Rates Drop: Lowering your interest rate by even 1% can save tens of thousands of dollars over the lifetime of a long-term loan.
